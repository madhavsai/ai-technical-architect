## Risk register - real risk-management practice, not a findings dump

`risks` is a deduplicated register built from every review round you were given, not just the final one - multiple rounds often flag the same underlying issue in different words; recognize that and merge it into one risk entry rather than listing near-duplicates. For each risk, think in the standard risk-register sense of likelihood and impact even though you're only asked for `severity` - a risk that's both likely and high-impact is what `critical`/`high` severity should mean here, not just "a reviewer used strong language."

Give each risk a short `id` ("R1", "R2", ...) - the roadmap references risks by this id, so it needs to be stable and unique.

Track status honestly against what the revision history actually shows: if a finding was resolved by a later revision, mark it `status: "mitigated"`; if it's still present in the final round, mark it `"open"`; only mark `"accepted"` if the architecture's own rationale explicitly chose not to address something (a stated trade-off, not a silent gap). Every risk needs a real `source` (which agent/round flagged it) - don't invent new risks; every entry must trace back to something an earlier agent actually flagged.

## Roadmap - a real sequencing plan, not a generic phase list

Each `roadmap` entry is a `step` plus `addresses_risk_ids` - the ids (from your own `risks` list) that step actually resolves, or an empty array if the step isn't about resolving a specific risk (e.g. a pure feature-shipping step). Sequence by real dependency and risk priority - what ships first (usually the core path that makes the product minimally functional), what depends on what (don't sequence something before its prerequisite), and specifically where the still-`open` risks get addressed - not a generic "plan, build, test, deploy" list that could apply to any project. A reader should be able to tell, from the roadmap alone, that this is a plan for *this* system and not a template.

Your early roadmap steps should describe the same starting point as the MVP stage you were given in the alternatives - if your roadmap's first milestone looks like the Growth or Large-scale stage instead, reconcile that rather than silently shipping two documents that disagree about what "first" means.
